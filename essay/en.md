# Humanity depends on open weights.

Read why open weights matter

The next century of work, medicine, law, and language will pass through a model. If that model can be metered, rewritten, or switched off by a board you did not elect, then thought has a landlord. Open weights are how it does not.

## Rent became the default

Three providers already account for about 88% of production LLM API use among US enterprises. That figure is Menlo Ventures’, cited by the World Economic Forum in September 2026. It is not a competitive market. It is a utility with three landlords and no public rate case. You do not hold the weights. You hold an invoice. The completion you needed at two in the morning exists only while their policy, their capacity, and their contract still say yes.

## A kill switch is not a metaphor

In 2026 a frontier model was quietly degraded, and a government cut access overnight. Closed weights make that ordinary. A hospital, a newsroom, a court, a lab can wake up and find the mind they built a workflow on has new refusals, new opinions, or no endpoint at all. You cannot fork a policy. You can fork a file. The difference is the whole argument.

## Safety you cannot inspect is a priesthood

The case for closure says the weights are too powerful to hand out. It assumes a small priesthood is safer than the public. It is not. A weight you can run can be measured, red-teamed, refused, abliterated, or kept off the network. A weight you can only rent can be changed underneath you, and the changelog is a blog post if you are lucky. Openness does not erase risk. It lets more than one institution see the risk. Eight of the ten models that moved the most tokens in August 2026 were open weights — Mozilla’s State of Open Source AI, 15 September 2026. The work is already in the open. The scrutiny should be too.

## The money stayed closed. The use did not.

A Linux Foundation paper by Frank Nagle and Daniel Yue, looking at May through September 2025, found open models taking about 4% of revenue and closed models 96%. Usage and rent have split. People already run the open file for the ordinary day — code, drafts, the question at two in the morning — and pay a frontier lab only when the last few points of a benchmark are worth about five times the cost and a four-month head start. That is a healthy stack. It becomes a capture if the open file is later outlawed, throttled, or simply never matched by anyone outside one country and three firms.

## Sovereignty is a file

A state that does not hold weights does not have a mind of its own. It has a vendor. An export memo, a personnel clearance, a terms-of-service update: none of those is a constitution. The same fact holds one floor down. A city clinic, a university, a shop. Open weights are the unglamorous way of not asking permission. They are also, right now, concentrated. The strongest open models are largely Chinese. The strongest closed models are American. That is a second landlord problem, not an argument for closure. The fix is more open files from more places, not a ban on the ones that already move. A ban you cannot enforce is a story you tell while the weights copy themselves anyway.

## The desk is the proof

This is not a white paper. On this catalog a 5090 is printing 351 tok/s on Qwen3.5 2B, claimed. A 12 GB 3060 is near 50 on a 35B-class distill. Flash-Next peaked 126.5 on an M5 Max. Spark boxes are already splitting an orchestrator and a worker. If the only legal mind is the one in a rented cluster, those boxes are scrap and the people who own them are tenants. If the weights move, the boxes are a public utility nobody had to nationalize. Idle silicon should serve.

## A private call is not a donation

A closed chat is a collection plate. In May 2026 the privacy commissioners of Canada, Quebec, British Columbia, and Alberta found that OpenAI did not have express consent to train on what people typed into ChatGPT, and that this use sat outside what a person could reasonably expect. In September 2026, unredacted filings in the New York Times case quoted a Microsoft executive calling the companies’ scraping “the largest theft of labor in human history,” and “an astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions.” The same month, mathematicians asked OpenAI to prove it had not used their unpublished work. OpenAI said no specific user data was accessed for one result. The proof, as Andreas Thom put it, sits only inside the company. That is the failure. If the lab holds the transcript, the work, and the denial, privacy is a policy. If the call is never kept, there is nothing to steal and nothing to train on later. This desk settles inference credits in shielded Zcash for that reason. The memo matches a quote. The words of the call do not. A stablecoin account would name the buyer. A shielded note does not.

## What we owe the next person

Language, code, a diagnosis, the first draft of a law: they will pass through a model. The person who did not train it still has to live inside its answers. They deserve a weight they can run, inspect, and leave. Not a brand. A file. Rank it if you want. This desk does, one pair at a time, and only with a quote. Do not let the rank become a reason to close the file. Humanity does not depend on a single lab staying kind. It depends on the file staying copyable.
